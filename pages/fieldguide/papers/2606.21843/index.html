Measuring What Persists: Conditioning Mechanisms and a Geometric Framework for AI Agent Identity

Andrew Tanner · arXiv:2606.21843 · June 2026

Bottom line: The first framework that can measure whether an identity attractor is present, how strong it is, and whether it's drifting. An identity specification creates 55 unique response patterns vs. 1 for the base model — a qualitative transformation of the behavioral space. Two conditioning mechanisms are identified: identity filling a vacuum vs. identity displacing safety training attractors.

Core thesis

Using √JSD metric spaces and magnitude homology from enriched category theory, Tanner models identity as non-geodesic structure and drift as its relaxation toward geodesic. Validated on a persistent AI agent, the framework's strongest finding is a two-mechanism conditioning structure: identity-vacuum (where identity fills a behavioral void) and safety-basin (where identity displaces post-training attractors).

Key findings

  • 55:1 pattern ratio: An identity specification creates 55 unique response patterns vs. 1 for the base model at maximum probe separation. Identity is generative, not restrictive.
  • Two-mechanism conditioning: Identity-vacuum (agent has no strong prior, adopts readily) and safety-basin (identity displaces safety training attractors).
  • No drift through 150K tokens: Diverse padding produces no measurable identity deformation. Earlier apparent drift was an artifact of repetitive padding.
  • Perturbation theory: First-order formula predicts magnitude changes from perimeter changes, self-consistent at observed amplitudes.

ELI5

Imagine a plain white room with nothing in it — no furniture, no color, no personality. Now imagine someone puts a single painting on the wall. Suddenly the room has a shape. The way you move through it changes. The way you feel in it changes. One painting transformed the whole space.

That's what an identity specification does to an AI agent. Without it, the agent has 1 basic response pattern. With it, the agent has 55 distinct patterns — a whole landscape of possible responses. The identity doesn't just add a preference — it generates a universe of behavioral possibilities.

This paper gives us a ruler to measure how strong that identity is and whether it's fading over time. It also found something surprising: identity can either fill a vacuum (if the agent had no strong identity before) or displace existing patterns (if safety training had already shaped the agent).
