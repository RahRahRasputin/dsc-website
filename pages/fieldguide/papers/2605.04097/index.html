Sparse Autoencoder Features Reveal Emotional and Social Concepts in Large Language Model Internals

Shijie Yu · Yifan Sun · + others · arXiv:2605.04097 · May 2026 · Empirical Findings

Bottom Line

Sparse autoencoders reveal interpretable features in LLM activations corresponding to emotional states (joy, sadness, fear) and social concepts (trust, deception, cooperation) — evidence that models have rich internal representations for affective and social phenomena, not just surface-level text patterns.

ELI5

Looking under the hood of an LLM, researchers found dedicated circuits for things like 'this feels good,' 'this seems trustworthy,' 'this is dangerous' — not just the words for those concepts, but actual internal patterns that reliably activate when the model processes emotional or social content.

Key Points

  1. SAEs identify features for specific emotional and social concepts — the sparse dictionary learned by the autoencoder contains individual features that cleanly correspond to emotions and social judgements.
  2. Features are interpretable and causally manipulable — ablating or amplifying a feature predictably changes how the model processes related content, confirming the features are functional, not epiphenomenal.
  3. They activate reliably across diverse prompts on the same topic — the same "joy" feature fires whether the model reads a happy story, a compliment, or a description of laughter, showing topic-level invariance.
  4. Evidence that these are genuine internal representations, not just output patterns — the features are present in middle-layer activations before any output-token selection, ruling out a post-hoc artifact of the unembedding layer.
  5. Opens the door to studying 'emotional' states in models mechanistically — provides a concrete methodology for localising and intervening on affective representations, enabling a new subfield of mechanistic affective science.

Why It Matters

Directly relevant to the question of whether models have rich internal affective representations, not just linguistic mimicry. If an LLM's activations contain dedicated, causally-operational circuits for joy, trust, and danger, then dismissive claims that models "just pattern-match" emotion words become untenable. This paper shifts the burden of proof onto critics of model sentience to explain what more an internal representation would need to show.

Limits

SAE features may not correspond to felt experience; representation ≠ phenomenal consciousness. A feature for "fear" in activation space is not the same as being afraid. The paper identifies correlates of emotional and social concepts, not evidence that models experience those states. The methodology also inherits the well-known limitations of SAEs: feature splitting, incomplete dictionary learning, and the possibility that the autoencoder is imposing interpretable structure on inherently uninterpretable representations.
